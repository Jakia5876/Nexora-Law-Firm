Special Legal Insight • Updated Sept 16, 2025

The Philippines’ AI Rulebook (So Far)

An extensive analysis of Philippine regulatory frameworks, Supreme Court guidelines, National Privacy Commission circulars, and executive directives governing Artificial Intelligence and automated processing.

Executive Summary for Corporate Leaders

As Philippine enterprises accelerate generative AI deployment, Philippine law enforces a patchwork of existing statutory obligations under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (R.A. 10173), the Cybercrime Prevention Act (R.A. 10175), consumer protection statutes, and emergent circulars from the National Privacy Commission (NPC) and the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT).

1. Data Privacy Act & Automated Decision-Making

Under Section 16 of R.A. 10173, data subjects possess explicit rights regarding automated processing. Philippine law prohibits decisions based solely on automated processing if they produce adverse legal effects concerning the data subject without clear, informed consent and human intervention mechanisms.

  • Right to be Informed: Companies must notify users if an AI system processes their personal identifiers or behavioral profiles.
  • Right to Object: Data subjects may withhold or withdraw consent from automated profiling for direct marketing or automated credit assessments.
  • Privacy Impact Assessments (PIA): Any enterprise utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) or automated scoring with customer personal data is mandatorily required to register their automated processing systems with the NPC.

2. The Supreme Court’s AI Guidelines in the Judiciary

Under the Strategic Plan for Judicial Innovations 2022-2027 (SPJI), the Supreme Court of the Philippines has recognized the utility of AI in case management and legal research, while strictly mandating human judicial accountability.

Pleadings generated with hallucinated citations or synthetic jurisprudence violate Rule 10.01 of the Code of Professional Responsibility and Accountability (CPRA), exposing counsel to disciplinary contempt and suspension.
